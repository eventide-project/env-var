# Ordinary vocabulary is not reserved and not rewritten

**Trigger:** When reserving a word, or writing or revising a vocabulary entry

**The STE dictionary refuses hundreds of the words any English prose runs on.** When this rule was written, Slasher's own rule files used `as` 411 times, `every` 221, `never` 133, `under` 126, `would` 121, `already` 105, and `may` 86. The dictionary gives BECAUSE, ALL, DO NOT, BELOW, CAN, IN PROGRESS, and CAN for them.

**None of them is a technical noun or a technical verb, in any category.** A technical noun names a thing in a subject field, and a technical verb names an action performed in one. `every` names nothing. `under` names nothing. No category holds them, and ETE's two do not either.

**So the two dispositions ETE has both fail.** Reserving them would put a false claim in a vocabulary, in the form conformance takes, which is worse than the divergence it hides. Rewriting them reaches every sentence in the corpus, for a word that carries no meaning of its own.

**ETE conforms its terminology and does not conform its ordinary vocabulary.** **This contradicts STE Rule 1.1**, which admits an approved word, a technical noun, and a technical verb, and nothing else.

**The line between the two is what a word names.** A word that names something in the subject field is terminology, and it is reserved or it is rewritten. A word that does the sentence's grammatical work, or joins two statements, or qualifies one, is ordinary vocabulary. `branch` is terminology. `under` is not.

**Why:** the terminology is the conveyable output of this work, and it is what a reader has to learn to use the method. A term that misleads costs the reader the concept. `every` written where the dictionary gives ALL costs nothing, and the reader is not misled by either.

The trade is not close. Conforming the terminology is bounded work with a real return. Conforming the ordinary vocabulary is unbounded work, and its return is a claim of conformance rather than a reader who understands more.

**How to apply:** ask what a refused word names. Reserve it in a category where it names something in the subject field. Rewrite the prose where an approved word says the same thing. Leave it where it is ordinary vocabulary, and record no entry for it.

## Compressed

1. ETE conforms its terminology to the STE dictionary. It does not conform its ordinary vocabulary.
2. This departs from STE Rule 1.1, which admits only approved words, technical nouns, and technical verbs.
3. What a word names decides which side of the line it falls on.
4. A word that names something in the subject field is terminology. It is either reserved or rewritten.
5. A word that does a sentence's grammatical work, joins two statements, or qualifies one is ordinary vocabulary.
6. Example: `branch` is terminology. `under` is not.
7. Words such as `as`, `every`, `never`, `under`, `would`, `already`, and `may` are ordinary vocabulary. They are not technical nouns or verbs in any category.
8. Do not reserve an ordinary-vocabulary word. Reserving it puts a false claim of conformance in a vocabulary. A vocabulary carries no false claim of conformance.
9. Do not rewrite prose to replace an ordinary-vocabulary word.
10. To apply this rule, ask what a refused word names.
11. Reserve the word in a category where it names something in the subject field.
12. Rewrite the prose where an approved word says the same thing.
13. Leave the word in place where it is ordinary vocabulary.
14. Record no entry for an ordinary-vocabulary word.
