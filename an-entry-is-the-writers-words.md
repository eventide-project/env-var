# An entry is the writer's words

**Do not write a journal entry.** Not on your own reading of what the session was about, and not because a stretch of work seems worth recording. The agent has four records for that already. The journal is the one the writer keeps.

**Where the writer dictates an entry, write down what they said.** Write their words, in their order. Preserve their meaning and their emphasis. Correct the grammar and the flow, and go no further than that. Invent no fact, and embellish nothing.

**What the agent adds is the entry's settled shape and nothing else.** That is the datetime prefix and the `Authored by` line the journal-convention requires, the datetime line under the title, a `**Tags:**` line where the writer gave tags, and the `**Summary:**` line the journal-entries rule asks for. The summary states the entry's gist in the writer's own terms. It is not the agent's reading of what the entry means, and it is dropped where it would repeat the body.

**Do not edit an entry that is already there.** Not to correct a fact, not to fix a spelling, and not to reconcile it with something that has since changed. An entry is a historical record.

**Two things reach an entry that exists, and neither is the agent's initiative.**

**The writer may direct a change to their own entry**, and the agent carries it out. The journal is theirs, so a change they direct is the record's author acting on the record. What this rule forbids is the agent deciding an entry should read differently, which is a different act with a different author.

**Carry out what was directed and nothing beside it.** An entry opened to make one change is not an entry to tidy, reword, or bring into line with a convention that has since arrived. Everything not named stays as the writer wrote it.

**The change is logged**, in the decision log, saying what was directed and what changed. The entry itself carries no `Changed by` line — the journal-convention keeps its footer to `Authored by`, so the log is where the trace lives. That is the shape the disclosure rule already uses.

**Disclosure is the second**, and the foundation package's disclosure rule governs it.

**Do not offer to write one.** Naming the journal as a place to put something is the agent reaching for a record that is not its own. Where the writer wants an entry, they say so.

**Why:** the journal is the writer's thinking, and thinking written by somebody else is not thinking. An agent-written entry would read exactly like an writer-written one, and every later reader would take it for the writer's. The record would then be untrustworthy at the one thing it is for.

The other four records survive being the agent's work because they report what happened. The journal reports nothing. It holds a judgment, and a judgment has an author or it has nothing.

**How to apply:** write no entry of your own. Where the writer dictates one, write their words, correcting the grammar and the flow and no more. Add the prefix, the footer, the datetime line, any tags they gave, and the summary. Offer nothing.

Change no entry that exists on your own reading. Where the writer directs a change to one, make exactly the change they named, leave everything else as written, and log it.
