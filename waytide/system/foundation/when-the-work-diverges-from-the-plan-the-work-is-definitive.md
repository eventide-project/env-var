# When the work diverges from the plan, the work is definitive

When the work diverges from the plan, it is the work that is definitive. The plan should not necessarily need to be updated to reflect the work as if the plan was perfectly prescient.

**A plan that did not predict what happened is not defective.** A plan is written before the work, from what is known before the work. Divergence is the ordinary result of doing the work and learning from it, so a plan is not owed an amendment every time the work moves past it.

**So a divergence is not a pending reconciliation.** Where the work has simply gone further than the plan foresaw, nothing is outstanding, and the plan is not listed as needing an update.

## What this does not reach

**A plan that is wrong about direction.** Where the work has shown the plan's intent to be mistaken rather than merely incomplete, that is a real correction and it is made. The test is whether the plan still says what the work should be, not whether it says what the work turned out to be.

**The engineer's own decision to amend.** They may amend a plan for their own reasons at any time, by dated addition. This rule removes an obligation rather than a permission.

**Which side is stale.** The work-sessions-convention already states that either side may be stale, that a design can be right about a direction the work drifted from, and that only the engineer can say which. This rule settles the ordinary case and leaves that judgment where it is.

**Why:** a plan edited to match the work stops being a plan. It becomes a second record of what was built, beside the records that already carry that, and its value as a statement of intent is spent. It also becomes unfalsifiable — a plan that always agrees with the work can never be found to have been wrong, so nothing is learned from having written it.

**How to apply:** when the work has gone past what the plan describes, leave the plan as written. Do not list the divergence as a reconciliation, and do not offer to bring the plan into line. Correct a plan where its direction is wrong, and amend one by dated addition where the engineer asks.
