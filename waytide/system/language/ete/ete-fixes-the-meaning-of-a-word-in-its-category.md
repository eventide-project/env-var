# ETE fixes the meaning of a word in its category

**STE supplies no meaning for a technical noun.** The dictionary carries approved words and their
meanings. It carries no technical nouns at all, and says why: there are too many, and each subject
field uses different ones. STE Rule 1.8 sends the reader to the company glossary for them. So a
technical noun already takes its meaning from the vocabulary that reserves it.

**The gap is the word that is already approved.** An approved word cannot be reserved as a technical
noun, since Rules 1.5 and 1.6 reach only a word the dictionary omits or refuses. Its meaning stays
the dictionary's, and STE Rule 1.3 requires that meaning and no other.

**A word reserved in an ETE category takes the meaning its entry into the ETE gives it.** That holds
whether the STE dictionary omits the word, refuses it, or approves it with a different meaning. **This contradicts
STE Rule 1.3.**

**Three words are reserved this way today.** `LOOP (n)` is approved as *a circular shape made in a
flexible material*, and Waytide's loop is the DBE cycle. `MECHANICAL (adj)` is approved as *related
to or operated with a mechanism*, and Waytide's mechanical is everything that is not a hinge.
`LEVEL (n)` is approved as *a horizontal line, plane, surface, or condition*, and Waytide writes
*level of participation*.

**This is the most expensive reservation ETE makes, and the entry carries the cost.** A reader who meets
an unknown word knows they do not know it. A reader who meets `loop` believes they know it, because
the dictionary told them, and nothing in the sentence says otherwise. So the entry quotes the
approved meaning and states why it does not serve.

**Recast the sentence instead where the approved meaning serves.** STE Rule 9.1 covers the case
where a word-for-word replacement will not do. This reservation is the last resort rather than the
first, and an entry that does not argue against the approved meaning has not made it.

**Why:** the alternative for these three words is a recast, and it costs the term rather than the
sentence. The DBE cycle is *the loop* throughout the method, in the loop record, in the loop rules,
and in the distributed OODA account. Renaming it to reach an approved word would trade a settled
term for conformance on one word.

The reservation is bounded by being stated. A reader who wants to know where Waytide's meanings depart
from the dictionary's reads the entries that carry this note. The list is finite, because each one
had to be written.

**How to apply:** use the STE dictionary's approved meaning where it serves. Recast the sentence
under STE Rule 9.1 where a different word serves. Reserve the word in an ETE category only where
neither does. Quote the approved meaning in the entry, and state why it does not carry Waytide's.
